Sports Massage vs
Deep Tissue: What's Actually Different.

Published 15 June 2026 · Updated 3 August 2026

Both use firm, targeted pressure, which is why the two get confused constantly. The real difference is in the goal and the timing, not just how hard the therapist presses.

Sports massage: built around activity

Sports massage is timed around your training — either before an event to prep the muscles, or after to aid recovery. Technique varies depending on which phase you're in: pre-workout sessions are often lighter and faster to increase blood flow, while post-workout sessions focus on flushing lactic acid and reducing soreness.

Deep tissue: built around chronic tension

Deep tissue isn't tied to a workout schedule at all — it's for tension that's built up over time, regardless of activity level. A desk worker with tight shoulders benefits from deep tissue just as much as an athlete. Sessions are typically slower and more sustained than sports massage.

Which one do you actually need?

If you're training for something specific and want a session timed around it, ask for sports massage. If you have ongoing tension unrelated to a training schedule — desk posture, stress, an old injury — deep tissue is usually the better match.

Can they overlap?

Yes — a lot of therapists trained in one are competent in both, and some sessions blend elements of each. If you're unsure, mention both what's bothering you and your activity level when you request a match, and we'll route you to the closer fit.
